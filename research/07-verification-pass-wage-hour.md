## 1. New York spread of hours — 12 NYCRR 142-2.4 (Miscellaneous Industries)

| Claim | Finding | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 142-2.4 text | Excerpt of the official Part 142 order: *"An employee shall receive one hour's pay at the basic minimum hourly wage rate, in addition to the minimum wage required in this Part for any day in which: (a) the spread of hours exceeds 10 hours; or (b) there is a split shift; or (c) both situations occur."* 142-2.18: spread of hours = *"the interval between the beginning and end of an employee's workday ... includes working time plus time off for meals plus intervals off duty."* 142-2.17: split shift = non-consecutive working hours; a meal period of one hour or less does not break consecutiveness **[142-2.17 wording from prior knowledge]**. | https://dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage-order-miscellaneous-industries-and-occupations-cr142 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-142-2.4 | confirmed |
| Contrast with hospitality | 146-1.6(d): *"This section shall apply to all employees in restaurants and all-year hotels, regardless of a given employee's regular rate of pay."* Part 142 has **no** such clause — the textual basis for the Misc-industries offset. | https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/12-NYCRR-146-1.6 | confirmed |
| Owed to ALL employees, or only when pay is below the minimum-wage floor? | NYSDOL's published position (opinion letters) and the majority of courts: for Part 142 employers the hour is owed **only to the extent weekly wages fall below the minimum-wage floor for all hours plus the spread/split hours**. Secondary sources quote the Department's call-in-pay letter RO-09-0133: *"if the amount paid to an employee for the workweek exceeds the minimum and overtime rate for the number of hours worked and the minimum wage rate for any call-in pay owed, no additional payment ... is required during that workweek"*, and state NYSDOL applies the same weekly test to spread of hours. Courts: *Seenaraine v. Securitas* (2d Dep't 2007) deferred to the Department's reading; *Zubair v. EnTech* (S.D.N.Y. 2011) and *Sosnowy v. A. Perri Farms* (E.D.N.Y. 2011) followed it **[case names from prior knowledge]**; *Doo Nam Yang v. ACBL* (S.D.N.Y.) declined to defer to the April 12, 2006 letter. **I could not retrieve RO-08-0059 or RO-09-0133 themselves** — the letter numbers are not verified; the quoted text is from a desk reference/commentary. | https://www.nycourts.gov/ctapps/Decisions/2019/Mar19/11-12opn19-Decision.pdf (deference discussion) ; https://www.probonopartner.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/New-York-Desk-Reference-with-Cover-February-2023.pdf ; https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/increased-new-york-minimum-wage-come-modified-obligations-under-state-wage-orders | likely |
| Daily or weekly test? | Commentators split (Jacobs Legal describes a daily test; the desk reference and Jackson Lewis a weekly test). The Department's own letters are framed per **workweek**. | as above | likely (weekly) |
| 2016/2017 amendment | The 31 Dec 2016 Part 142 amendments restructured the minimum-wage and allowance schedules by region; 142-2.4 kept the words "in addition to the minimum wage required in this Part" and did **not** add a "regardless of regular rate" clause. **[amendment text not re-verified — no primary page reachable]** | https://forms.labor.ny.gov/WP/CR142.pdf | likely |

**Interpretation the app should use.** Apply the NYSDOL weekly offset (the legally defensible floor), and offer a per-tenant "always pay" switch for owners who want the conservative hospitality-style treatment. Never use a daily test as the *only* test, but a daily check is the stricter of the two, so it is acceptable as the "always pay / strict" option.

**Exact formula (per workweek, per employee):**

```
MW   = basic minimum wage for the region and year (NYC/LI/W $17.00; rest of state $16.00 in 2026 and 2027)
H    = total hours worked in the workweek
OT   = max(0, H - 40)
D    = number of workdays on which spread > 10h OR a split shift occurred (count a day once even if both)
W    = all wages paid for the week for hours worked: hourly/day-rate/commission straight time + overtime premium;
       exclude tips, exclude any spread/call-in pay already paid
floor = MW*(H - OT) + 1.5*MW*OT + MW*D
spread_pay_owed = max(0, floor - W)
```

**Engine check (`engine.ts` lines 243-262).** The app computes `floorWithSpread = MW*(hours + longDays)` and compares it with `straightTimeCents` only. Because it omits the overtime premium from *both* sides, and the premium actually paid (0.5 × regular rate × OT) is never less than 0.5 × MW × OT, the app's shortfall is **≥ the Department's figure in every case and equal to it when there is no overtime**. That is compliant and slightly generous — acceptable. Two gaps: (1) **split shifts are not detected** (only `spreadMinutes > 600`); add a split-shift flag per 142-2.17 (a non-meal gap > 60 min between punches); (2) the fallback `d.spreadMinutes ?? d.minutes` under-counts spread when a day has only worked minutes — always store first-in/last-out. The `rule` string should cite `12 NYCRR 142-2.4, 142-2.17, 142-2.18; NYSDOL weekly-offset opinion letters`.
