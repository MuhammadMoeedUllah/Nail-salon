# 10. Open questions for the pilot salons and remaining market gaps

**Scope.** Answers, with evidence, to the three open questions in `docs/PLAN.md` section 10 (turn board, pay cadence, card tips paid in cash) and to the five "unverified" or follow-up items left open in research files 01 and 06 (NYC salon count and task-force results, booth-rent prevalence, tip-theft and wage lawsuits 2023-26, supply deductions, software pricing).

**Method and limits.** Thirty-six web searches in English and Vietnamese on 2026-10-07. The sandbox proxy blocked every direct page fetch (data.ny.gov, squareup.com, support.gusto.com, irs.gov, mangoforsalon.com, abturns.com, the Vietnamese blogs), so each figure comes from a search excerpt of the linked page. Reddit threads did not surface for any of six phrasings; the tech-voice evidence is Vietnamese blogs, YouTube/TikTok titles and vendor copy. Confidence: High (primary source, excerpt on point), Medium (secondary or partial excerpt), Low (single vendor or blog claim).

**Recommendation: optional, default ON for ticket counts, OFF for dollars.** Every described dispute traces to an invisible or discretionary rule (a skipped walk-in, a mid-day re-sort by income, a senior tech running the sheet), and every fix on offer, from the Neo Nails history log to ABTurns, is more visibility, not less. What techs object to is being compared by earnings, which the Vietnamese advice columns call out explicitly. So the day view should show each technician's ticket count for today (a fact already on the paper sheet and in every tech's head), with a per-salon toggle to hide it, and never show service dollars or tips on the shared screen; those stay on the tech's own stub. Do not build "turns" (half turns, request rules, income re-sort) into v1: they are policy, they differ by salon, and the pilot salons should be asked which of NIA's three regimes they run before anything is coded.

**Recommendation: keep weekly as the default; allow biweekly behind a one-line warning for New York.** Weekly is both the community custom (every ad is "/tuần") and the statutory rule for a worker the state would almost certainly classify as manual. The product should default the pay period to Monday-Sunday weekly, let the owner pick biweekly with the note "NY requires weekly pay for most salon staff", and keep the settlement weekday configurable because no source, English or Vietnamese, names one.

**Recommendation.** The $99/month tier is the price of a full POS with check-in and card processing, and it comes with setup fees and term commitments that owners resent. A pay-records layer that does not process cards should sit well under that: a flat $29-49 per location per month, no per-technician fee, no setup fee, month to month, with a free tier capped by weeks of history rather than by features. The three things reviewers punish, free-to-paid bait, per-staff pricing and fee opacity, are the three things to promise against in the first sentence of the pricing page.
