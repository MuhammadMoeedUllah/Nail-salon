# 11 — UI/UX patterns and evidence for the interface revamp

Research date: 2026-10-08. Scope: what visual cues, colour, placement, navigation, input and feedback patterns measurably help people use a shared-tablet clock, a front-desk ticket log and a weekly pay review, when the people are Vietnamese-American salon owners and technicians using an iPad, a cheap Android tablet or a phone. Twenty-eight web searches (standard mode) on 2026-10-08. The sandbox proxy blocks full-page fetches (nngroup.com, w3.org and the vendor help centres all failed to resolve), so figures come from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages and from secondary write-ups; those are marked **[secondhand]**. Thin or vendor-only evidence is marked **[weak]**; our own reasoning is **[inference]**. Builds on research 06 (user voices, §5-6) and 09 (minimum clicks); the design rules at the end (R1-R30) are what `docs/UX-REVAMP-PLAN.md` cites.

## 1. Visual cues: how people know what to tap

- **Weak signifiers cost time.** NN/g's eyetracking study of "flat" pages found weak-signifier versions took 22% longer on average and drew about 25% more fixations than strong-signifier versions (paired t-test, p < 0.05); longer viewing meant hesitation, not engagement. The penalty shrinks on sparse pages with consistent layout where the key controls stand out. Ghost buttons and link text styled as plain text were the weak cases ([NN/g, Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/); [NN/g response to criticism](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-criticisms-flat-design/)). Implication: filled primary buttons with borders or shadows, underlined links, raised tiles on the kiosk.
- **Icons need words.** NN/g's "Icon Usability" (Harley, 2014) is the reference every design system cites; the NHS Turas system keeps label-free icons to home, print, search and close, and asks that text sit next to its icon ([Turas icons](https://www.turasdesignsystem.nhs.scot/design/icons/); [NN/g article, not fetched](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)) **[secondhand]**. Secondary write-ups report icon-only navigation scoring below icon-plus-label in comprehension tests, worst for non-standard icons ([Web Designer Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/why-icon-only-design-is-failing-users-the-case-for-text-labels); [BC Gov icon guidance](https://classic.developer.gov.bc.ca/Icons)) **[weak]**. Implication: every icon in the app carries a visible label in the active language; emoji glyphs (which render differently per device) are replaced by one icon set.
- **Show system status, fast.** NN/g's first heuristic: keep people informed with appropriate feedback within a reasonable time; even a colour change after a press counts, and a button should keep its pressed look until the screen changes so people do not tap twice ([NN/g visibility of system status](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/); [Zebra mobile UI mistakes](https://developer.zebra.com/community/home/blog/2015/08/05/mobile-ui-mistakes-to-avoid-the-touch-target)).
- **Response-time limits.** 0.1 s feels instant, 1 s keeps the train of thought, 10 s is the limit of attention; beyond one second show an indicator ([NN/g, Response Times: 3 Important Limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/); [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/3-response-time-limits-interaction-design/)). The Doherty threshold (about 400 ms, IBM 1982) is the practitioner rule for acknowledging input ([uxtoast](https://www.uxtoast.com/ux-laws/doherty-threshold); [glossary caveat that it is a heuristic](https://atticusli.com/behavioral-science-glossary/doherty-threshold/)). A loader that flashes for a few dozen milliseconds reads as a glitch, so hold it 300-600 ms once shown ([Virtuslab](https://virtuslab.com/blog/software-development/ux-patterns-beyond-raw-performance)) **[weak]**.
- **Skeleton screens are not a free win.** Viget's test (about 39 people per group, GIF simulations) found skeleton screens were perceived as *slower* (2.82 s vs 2.41 s for spinners and 2.29 s blank) and fewer people agreed the content loaded quickly (59% vs 74%) ([Viget](https://www.viget.com/articles/a-bone-to-pick-with-skeleton-screens)). A smaller uxdesign.cc study found left-to-right shimmer felt shorter than pulsing, with tiny samples ([uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/what-you-should-know-about-skeleton-screens-a820c45a571a)) **[weak]**. Implication: use skeletons for layout stability on the pay week (so numbers do not jump), spinners-in-button for actions, nothing under 300 ms.
- **Optimistic updates** pay off only when failures are rare and cheap to roll back; never for payments or deletes ([Simon Hearne](https://simonhearne.com/2021/optimistic-ui-patterns); [137foundry](https://dev.to/137foundry/why-pessimistic-ui-is-still-the-right-call-for-destructive-actions-447j); [OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/optimistic-updates-make-apps-faster/)). Implication: adding a ticket may append the row immediately; approving a week or marking paid waits for the server.
- **Undo beats "Are you sure?"** Jakob Nielsen's error-prevention heuristic lists confirmation as one remedy but warns that overuse trains people to click through; Blender's HIG, Aza Raskin and Andy Matuschak all argue for undo wherever the action is reversible, with dialogs kept for data loss ([Blender HIG dialogs](https://developer.blender.org/docs/features/interface/human_interface_guidelines/dialogs/); [Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zXH4ASvHNz488hCNgrBjQJK); [InformIT](https://informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=1091575); [137foundry on choosing](https://dev.to/137foundry/how-to-choose-between-confirmation-dialogs-undo-windows-and-soft-delete-patterns-1opj)). Toast timing in design-system guidance: plain toasts 4-6 s, undo snackbars 6-10 s and they must carry the action; toasts are the wrong place for errors ([NN/g indicators, validations, notifications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/); [Carbon notification pattern](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/notification-pattern/)). Destructive-action guidance adds: put the confirm away from the trigger and avoid defaulting focus to it ([uxcel destructive actions](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/ux-design-psychology/the-nature-of-user-errors-902/confirm-destructive-actions-4778); [Primer confirmation dialog](https://primer.style/product/components/confirmation-dialog/guidelines)).
- **Gestalt for cards.** Proximity and common region group content; borders win over spacing, and gaps inside a card must be smaller than gaps between cards or the grouping breaks ([Laws of UX, common region](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-common-region/); [NN/g proximity video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/proximity-gestalt/); [uxcel common region](https://uxcel.com/blog/law-of-the-common-region-in-ux)).

## 2. Colour

- **Rules that bind.** WCAG 1.4.1 (Level A): colour is never the only way to convey status, a required field or a selected state; repeat it as text, an icon, a border or a shape ([W3C Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/use-of-color)). WCAG 1.4.3 (AA): text 4.5:1, large text (about 24 px regular or 18.7 px bold) 3:1; placeholder text counts as text ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/UNDERSTANDING-WCAG20/visual-audio-contrast-contrast.html)). WCAG 1.4.11 (AA): UI component boundaries and status icons 3:1 against adjacent colours; a light-grey field border measured 2.87:1 fails ([yatil, non-text contrast](https://www.yatil.net/blog/non-text-contrast-in-detail-ui-components); [tabnav 1.4.11](https://tabnav.com/academy/wcag/success-criterion-1.4.11)). Contrast tracks luminance, so two clearly different hues can still fail.
- **Colour vision.** About one in ten men has red-green deficiency ([weandthecolor fintech palette post](https://weandthecolor.com/color-psychology-in-fintech-branding-how-the-right-palette-builds-user-trust/209146)) **[secondhand]**. Okabe-Ito is the most cited colourblind-safe set; only its blue (#0072B2, about 5.2:1) passes 4.5:1 for text on white, so status hues need darker shades for text and the base hues for icons and borders ([AudioEye colourblind palettes](https://www.audioeye.com/post/colorblind-friendly-palettes/)). Overlaid glyphs (check, exclamation, slash) rescue a palette that is not fully safe ([Jenkins issue 66224](https://issues.jenkins-ci.org/browse/JENKINS-66224)).
- **Semantic tokens.** Name colours by role (success, warning, danger, info, with on-X foregrounds) rather than by hue so a palette can change in one place; hard-coded hex in components turns a later fix into a hunt ([Automatic.css semantic colours](https://docs.automaticcss.com/colors/semantic-colors); the same objection was raised by Bitcoin Core reviewers against hard-coded red and green, [PR 437 mirror](https://mirror.b10c.me/bitcoin-core-gui/437/)).
- **Blue for trust, green for money and go, red for problems.** Design-industry pieces repeat an Adobe survey in which 54% of people picked blue as the colour they trust most, and recommend blue as the anchor, green for action and confirmation, and red only for alerts ([weandthecolor](https://weandthecolor.com/color-psychology-in-fintech-branding-how-the-right-palette-builds-user-trust/209146); [Progress, colours for fintech](https://www.progress.com/blogs/how-choose-right-colors-fintech); [Windmill](https://windmill.digital/insights/psychology-of-color-in-financial-app-design)) **[weak, no primary study found]**. A payroll vendor's redesign moved to cool blues and whites for a "professional" feel ([Clear Books](https://www.clearbooks.co.uk/blog/improvements-to-our-payroll-application/)).
- **Red money is contested.** Monzo users pushed back on red outgoing amounts as alarming, suggesting a minus sign or red only for overdraft ([Monzo community](https://community.monzo.com/t/money-take-from-account-to-show-as-a-negative-value/88915)); Manager.io replaced red negatives with separate columns and keeps red for errors ([Manager forum](https://forum.manager.io/t/bug-in-import-bankstatement/17744)); Quicken colours inflows green and turns a balance red only when negative ([Quicken community](https://community.quicken.com/discussion/comment/20074359)); a full-row red for negatives was reverted as "overly aggressive" ([wxBanker bug](https://bugs.qastaging.launchpad.net/wxbanker/+bug/370573)). Chinese markets invert the convention (red = gain) ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2015/07/15/rules-governing-chinese-stock-market)); Vietnamese exchanges use green up and red down with purple and cyan for limits **[background, verify]**, and red is the colour of luck and money envelopes in Vietnamese culture, so red on a pay screen reads "attention", not "bad luck" **[inference]**. Implication: keep red for the one number the law adds ("owed by law"), always with a label and an icon, never for buttons except the confirm step of a destructive action; clock-out, void and unpair are not red.
- **Light mode wins for normal vision.** NN/g's review of the Piepenbrock studies: dark text on light backgrounds beat light-on-dark on acuity and proofreading for both younger and older adults; the advantage grew as type got smaller; people did not notice the difference even as they performed better; some low-vision readers (cataracts) may still prefer dark ([NN/g, Dark Mode vs. Light Mode](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/); [Solute Labs summary](https://blog.solutelabs.com/blog/light-vs-dark-mode)) **[secondhand]**. Salons are brightly lit; the app stays light, with an optional dimmed kiosk screen after hours only.

## 3. Placement and hierarchy

- **Fitts's law and the thumb.** Time to tap depends on distance and target size ([Laws of UX, Fitts](https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/using-fitts-law-guide-users-app)). Hoober's 1,333-observation field study: 49% hold the phone one-handed, 36% cradled, 15% two-handed; 67% of one-handed use is the right thumb ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php), via research 09). Thumb-zone maps put the bottom-centre in easy reach and the top corners out of reach on tall phones ([Smashing, The Thumb Zone](https://smashingmagazine.com/2016/09/the-thumb-zone-designing-for-mobile-users); [Parachute Design](https://parachutedesign.ca/blog/thumb-zone-ux/)).
- **One filled primary per screen.** Visa's design system limits the filled style to one action per screen; Apple's HIG lets the primary change per screen but caps it at one; WordPress's component docs warn two filled buttons fight for attention ([Visa button usage](https://design.visa.com/components/button/usage/); [uxcel on HIG primary buttons](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/apple-hig/system-buttons-actions-400/primary-action-buttons-1737); [WordPress Button](https://developer.wordpress.org/block-editor/components/Button/)).
- **Sticky bottom action bar** once content exceeds about 1.5 screen heights; not on short forms, never inside modals; pad the content so the bar never covers the last field, and check the keyboard does not cover the submit ([Toyota Europe sticky bottom bar](https://dx-playbook.toyota-europe.com/cds/modules/sticky-bottom-bar/); [Landing Doctors](https://landingdoctors.com/guides/mobile-landing-page-conversion); [Smashing, which PWA elements should stick](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2020/01/mobile-pwa-sticky-bars-elements/)). Vendor A/B numbers (+55% CTA clicks, +12% add-to-cart) are unverified ([AB Tasty](https://www.abtasty.com/blog/mobile-stick-to-scroll/)) **[weak]**.
- **Focus not obscured.** WCAG 2.2 2.4.11 (AA): sticky headers, bars and banners may not fully hide the focused control ([W3C What's new in 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/); [Deque 2.2 summary](https://dequeuniversity.com/resources/wcag-2.2)).
- **Dashboards.** A core view fits one screen with three to seven metrics; each view answers one question; the lead metric sits upper-left where reading starts; warning colour is reserved for the rare item that needs action; numbers carry a comparison or target ([TalTech dashboard lecture](https://courses.cs.taltech.ee/w/images/3/30/ITI0209_24_16_01_Dashboard.html.pdf); [Lazarev](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/dashboard-ux-design); [uxcel, simplify dashboards](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/kpi-okr-for-products/interpreting-and-exploring-metrics-687/simplify-dashboards-7271); [SCU data viz](https://blogs.scu.edu/dataviz/?p=955)). Owner home screens in our category: Toast Now is one date at a time with a "return to today" icon; Square's widget shows gross, net, transactions, average sale; Homebase shows paid hours, wages, sales, labour % and team status indicators; none shows money owed ([Toast Now](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Get-Started-with-the-Toast-Now-App); [Square dashboard app](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5618-get-started-with-the-square-dashboard-app); [Homebase labour cost](https://joinhomebase.com/features/labor-cost), via research 09).
- **iPad two-pane.** Landscape shows list and detail together (default about one-third / two-thirds); portrait leads with detail and hides the list behind an edge swipe, so headline numbers must not live only in the list column; full-width single columns look wrong on large displays ([Xojo split layout guide](https://docs.xojo.com/topics/user_interface/ios/creating_split_layout_for_larger_ios_screens.html); third-party iPadOS guideline pages) **[secondhand]**.
- **iOS input zoom.** Safari zooms the page when a focused input, textarea or select has a computed font size under 16 px, and does not zoom back out; 15.75 px still triggers it; the viewport `maximum-scale` workaround breaks accessibility ([West Wind](https://weblog.west-wind.com/posts/2023/Apr/17/Preventing-iOS-Textbox-Auto-Zooming-and-ViewPort-Sizing); [Telerik KB](https://docs.telerik.com/devtools/aspnet-ajax/knowledge-base/common-disable-autozoom-input-safari-iphone); [HeroUI issue](https://github.com/heroui-inc/heroui/issues/5326)). Implication: every input is 16 px or larger, including table-cell inputs.

## 4. Navigation

- **Hidden navigation costs discoverability.** NN/g's 179-person test: hidden (hamburger) navigation cut discoverability by about a fifth, raised perceived difficulty, and lengthened task time about 15% on phones and 39% on desktop ([NN/g hidden navigation methodology](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hidden-navigation-methodology/); [NN/g, don't use hamburger on desktop](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/find-navigation-desktop-not-hamburger/); [Smart Insights summary](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/are-hamburger-menus-hidden-navigation-good-for-ux/)) **[secondhand on the figures]**.
- **Bottom tab bars, three to five destinations.** Material and Apple both place primary destinations at the bottom with icons plus labels; a Waikato thesis found participants preferred a bottom bar to a top-left hamburger; Spotify reported +9% clicks overall and +30% on menu items after switching to a tab bar ([uxcel tab bar](https://uxcel.com/glossary/tab-bar); [Waikato thesis](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/entities/publication/3fa280bc-9c78-4449-b83c-6a39e93ee191); [Roselli relaying Spotify](https://adrianroselli.com/2016/01/avoid-the-hamburger-menu-for-desktop-layouts.html); [Smashing bottom navigation](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2019/08/bottom-navigation-pattern-mobile-web-pages); [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/should-all-mobile-navigation-be-at-the-bottom-4c434b5159cd)) **[Spotify figure secondhand and old]**. Hamburgers and "More" menus still suit settings, account and rarely used features ([uxcel hamburger](https://uxcel.com/glossary/hamburger-menu)).
- **Jakob's law.** People expect a product to work like the ones they already use; convention conserves attention for the task ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)). For this audience that means Square's tile grid and tip presets, Homebase's PIN flow, iOS passcode dots, and the phone's share sheet (Messages, Zalo).
- **Progressive disclosure.** Defer secondary features to a second screen; it eases learning and reduces errors; show what about 80% of people need 80% of the time with the rest one move away; two or three levels at most; never hide required fields, legal text or the consequences of destructive actions ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/); [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/progressive-disclosure/); [UX Tigers](https://uxtigers.com/post/progressive-disclosure); [concept wiki](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/progressive-disclosure/)). Hick's law (RT = a + b·log2 n) is the decision-time rationale, with the caveat that ordered lists are scanned, not weighed ([IxDF](https://ixdf.org/literature/article/the-hick-hyman-law-an-argument-against-complexity-in-user-interface-design); [Designorate](https://www.designorate.com/hicks-law-building-usable-navigations/)).
- **Consistent help and no redundant entry.** WCAG 2.2 3.2.6 (A): help links keep the same relative order on every page; 3.3.7 (A): information a person already gave in a flow is auto-filled or selectable ([W3C new in 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/); [TPGi on 3.3.7](https://tpgi.com/how-to-test-3-3-7-redundant-entry); [BOIA on 3.2.6](https://www.boia.org/blog/consistent-help-tips-for-meeting-wcag-success-criterion-3-2-6)).

## 5. Touch targets and the kiosk

- **Sizes.** WCAG 2.5.8 (AA) floor 24×24 CSS px or equivalent spacing; 2.5.5 (AAA) 44×44 ([W3C Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [DAISY KB](https://kb.daisy.org/publishing/docs/wcag/target-size-minimum.html)). Apple: 44×44 pt ([Deque attest iOS rule](https://dequeuniversity.com/rules/attest-ios/1.0/touch-target-size)) **[secondhand]**. Android: 48×48 dp with at least 8 dp between targets, about 9 mm; a 24 dp icon sits inside a 48 dp hit area ([Android accessibility help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). NN/g: about 1 cm square, in physical units ([NN/g touch target size](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)). Kiosk vendors ask for 20 mm buttons with 5 mm gaps ([kiosk.com UI guide](https://kiosk.com/kiosk-ui/)) and finger ranges of 9-12 mm ([AVIXA checklist](https://xchange.avixa.org/posts/kiosk-ux-ui-design-checklist)). A 2025 study on a 65-inch touchscreen found optimal buttons near 30 mm for younger and 40 mm for older users; a countertop tablet is far smaller, so treat as direction only ([HIT study](https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/exploring-usability-disparities-in-multi-touch-screen-interaction/)) **[weak]**. Implication: 48 px minimum everywhere, 56 px primaries, 64-72 px kiosk keys and tiles, 8 px gaps.
- **Kiosk behaviour.** Idle reset after 30-60 s with a visible countdown; one obvious next step per screen; back buttons; keep controls within reach and away from the top edge; no on-screen keyboards where a keypad will do; oversized keyboards let bystanders read input ([Kiosk Group idle limit](https://support.kioskgroup.com/article/991-idle-time-limit); [WhosOnLocation timeout](https://helpdesk.whosonlocation.com/hc/en-us/articles/203609860-Set-up-auto-timeout-for-a-kiosk); [Frank Mayer](https://www.frankmayer.com/blog/user-interface-design-for-kiosks/); [Axiell large touchscreens](https://www.axiell.com/blog-post/designing-for-large-touchscreen-displays); [uxcel self-service kiosks](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/design-foundations/intro-to-devices-676/self-service-kiosks-3005); [flyx kiosk UX](https://www.flyx.cloud/en/blog/effective-ux-ui-design-for-self-service-kiosks-best-practices-tips/)).
- **PIN pad layout.** The phone grid (1-2-3 on top) is the defensible default: Bell Labs testers found the calculator layout unintuitive; about 55% of people handed a blank grid put 1-2-3 across the top; Cambridge trials favoured 1-2-3; payment terminals standardise it (EN 1332-3, CEA-2041 with 0 under 8, a tactile dot on 5) ([99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/article/squaring-circle-seventen-telephone-keypad-layouts/); [shkspr on number pads](https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2023/06/on-the-usability-of-number-pads/); [uxdesign.cc on telephones](https://uxdesign.cc/what-telephones-can-tell-you-about-good-design-6c24885ca7e9); [Taylor & Francis chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/e/9781315098128/chapters/10.1201/b22361-50); [RNIB accessible chip and PIN](https://www.rnib.org.uk/living-with-sight-loss/money-and-benefits/accessible-chip-and-pin); [Verifone PIN keypad spec](https://docs.verifone.com/functional-specification/nav_pin_keypad); [EN 1332-3](https://www.evs.ee/en/evs-en-1332-3-2008); [CEA-2041](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/CEA/preview_CEA+2041+Final+2012.pdf)). Shuffled pads trade memorability for security and are wrong for a staff clock **[inference]**. Our current pad already uses this layout; keep it, make the keys larger, add shake and dot-clear on error.
- **Haptics and sound.** The web Vibration API is not implemented in iOS Safari, so tactile feedback on iPads must come from visual and audio cues; a short click sound via Web Audio is allowed after a user gesture **[background, verify]**.
- **Kiosk lockdown.** iPad: add to Home Screen (manifest `display: standalone`; Home Screen web apps get their own storage and no browser chrome), set Display Auto-Lock to Never, then Guided Access (Settings → Accessibility; triple-click side or Home button; Options can disable the sleep button and touch areas; exit needs the passcode or Face ID) ([Zenoti guided access](https://help.zenoti.com/en/kiosk/onboard-and-set-up/set-up-guided-access-for-kiosk-on-ipad.html); [Eptura](https://knowledge.eptura.com/Visitor/Apps/Eptura_Visitor_Kiosk/Set_up_Guided_Access_for_Apple_iPad); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/lock-ipad-guided-access/); [Humanforce kiosk on iPad](https://help.humanforce.com/hc/en-au/articles/7868263405327-How-do-I-setup-kiosk-an-an-Apple-iPad); [Apple WWDC23 web apps](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10120/); [Apple configuring web applications](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AppleApplications/Reference/SafariWebContent/ConfiguringWebApplications/ConfiguringWebApplications.html)). Android: Settings → Security → Screen pinning (name varies), require PIN to unpin, then Recents → app icon → Pin; it survives neither reboot nor the power button and is not anti-tamper; Android Enterprise kiosk mode is the stronger option ([Intuiface](https://support.intuiface.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015747711-Use-screen-pinning-to-create-kiosk-mode-on-Android); [ClockShark](https://help.clockshark.com/locking-your-android-tablet-or-smartphone-to-clocksharks-kioskclock); [Esper](https://www.esper.io/blog/what-is-android-screen-pinning-what-can-it-be-used-for); [Trio](https://www.trio.so/blog/android-kiosk-mode)). Manifest `display: fullscreen` hides system UI on Android Chrome and falls back to standalone elsewhere; browsers need a tap before entering fullscreen ([web.dev manifest](https://web.dev/articles/add-manifest); [Smashing, PWA display modes](https://smashingmagazine.com/2025/08/optimizing-pwas-different-display-modes/); [pwa-display-test](https://github.com/tecdrop/pwa-display-test)). Paid kiosk browsers (Kiosker, about $40) add allowlists ([Kiosker](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1446738885)).

## 6. Tables and numbers

- Right-align numeric columns and their headers; left-align text; never centre; use tabular (fixed-width) figures so digits line up; the payoff grows with table length ([Matthew Ström, Design better data tables](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/tables/); [uxcel numbers alignment](https://app.uxcel.com/courses/ui-components-best-practices/tables-best-practices-356/numbers-alignment-1984)). Noto Sans supports `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` **[background, verify in the build]**.
- Sticky headers suit phones because vertical scrolling is natural, but a wrapper with `overflow-x: auto` turns into the scroll container, so cap its height or the header pins to the wrapper; multi-line headers hide too much on small phones ([Ninja Tables](https://ninjatables.com/sticky-headers-vs-fixed-columns/); [LogRocket responsive tables](https://dev.to/logrocket/improving-responsive-data-table-ux-with-css-2ngi); [shadcn sticky block](https://www.shadcnblocks.com/block/data-table5)).
- Mobile patterns: horizontal scroll with a pinned first column (capped at 30% width) or conversion to object cells/cards ([SAP Fiori data table](https://experience.sap.com/fiori-design-android/data-table)). Light zebra striping and thin pale rules help the eye track wide rows; heavy fills hurt ([Inforiver](https://inforiver.com/wp-content/uploads/Best-Practices-for-table-design.pdf); [UXPin table UX](https://uxpin.com/studio/blog/table-ux)). Our pay week has 16 columns; on phones it must become per-technician cards with the three numbers that matter, with the full table kept for desktop and the bookkeeper **[inference]**.

## 7. Forms and input

- **Single column, labels above.** Baymard found 16% of checkouts use extensive multi-column layouts and that people skip or mis-enter fields in them; Smashing cites Google research for top-aligned labels, which also suit narrow screens; placeholders are not labels because they vanish on typing and fail contrast ([Baymard form design](https://baymard.com/learn/form-design); [Baymard checkout form best practices](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-form-best-practices); [Smashing mobile forms](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2018/08/best-practices-for-mobile-form-design); [uxcel alignment](https://app.uxcel.com/lessons/anatomy-516/alignment-6230); [madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/form-design-best-practices/)).
- **Inline validation after the field is complete.** The Wroblewski/Etre study (22 people, six variants): the best inline version raised success 22%, cut errors 22%, raised satisfaction 31% and cut completion time 42% versus submit-only; Baymard adds: validate on completion or blur, not mid-entry, clear the error once fixed, confirm good input, and never rely on red alone ([A List Apart inline validation](https://alistapart.com/article/inline-validation-in-web-forms/); [Baymard inline validation](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation); [Reform](https://www.reform.app/blog/validation-improves-form-performance); [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/ecommerce-checkout-best-practices)).
- **Phones show two or three fields at a time** because the keyboard takes about 40% of the screen; keep fields above the keyboard and auto-scroll ([Baymard via Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2018/08/best-practices-for-mobile-form-design); [Babich mobile forms](https://babich.biz/blog/mobile-form-usability/)).
- **Dropdowns last.** Design systems call the dropdown the "UI of last resort" and route two to five options to segmented controls or radios; Modus 2-5, Michelin 2-5 with no wrapping, GitLab 2-3 (max 5); dropdowns from five to seven options up; small screens are where dropdowns fail ([CMS design system](https://design.cms.gov/components/dropdown); [Ripple](https://ripple.sdp.vic.gov.au/design-system/components/dropdown); [Prism](https://prism.coxautoinc.com/docs/components/dropdowns/); [Modus segmented controls](https://modus.trimble.com/components/in-field/segmented-controls/); [Michelin](https://designsystem.michelin.com/components/segmented-buttons); [GitLab](https://design.gitlab.com/components/segmented-control); [NN/g dropdown list](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dropdown-list/); [Ontruck](https://nerds.ontruck.com/what-to-use-when-you-have-multiple-options-in-a-form-field-2c35887adda5); [Luke Wroblewski thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9915501)). Implement segmented controls as native radios in a fieldset ([Tabler](https://docs.tabler.io/ui/components/segmented-control.md)).
- **Money input.** `type="text"` with `inputmode="decimal"` and a two-decimal pattern; `type="number"` rounds on scroll, shows spinners and iOS may insert thousands separators on submit; a visible `$` prefix saves a keyboard layer; cents-first entry ("1111" → 11.11) needs no decimal key; store integer cents and format for display only ([Etch money input](https://etch.co/blog/money-input); [Brad Frost](https://v9.bradfrost.com/blog/post/better-numerical-inputs-for-mobile-forms); [Rocket Validator](https://rocketvalidator.com/html-validation/the-inputmode-attribute-is-not-supported-in-all-browsers-please-be-sure-to-test-and-consider-using-a-polyfill.md); [Tadabase thread](https://community.tadabase.io/t/set-inputmode-on-a-currency-field-to-decimal/208); [Cashtab keypad review](https://reviews.bitcoinabc.org/D20330)). Our `parseDollars` already stores cents; the tablet gains an on-screen keypad so the OS keyboard never covers the ticket builder **[inference]**.

## 8. Older adults, low digital literacy, low English

- **Type.** NN/g (via banking summaries) sets a 12 pt (16 px) floor with user-enlargeable text; most older users never find resize controls; WeChat's ageing standard asks 30 pt for main text; larger is not always better, a 2022 review found a threshold past which readability drops and a 2018 smartphone study found larger type raised errors ([The Financial Brand summarising NN/g](https://thefinancialbrand.com/news/digital-marketing-banking/banking-websites-for-baby-boomers-millennials-58890); [SitePoint thread on Hudson's research](https://www.sitepoint.com/community/t/older-web-users-and-text-size/76629); [WeChat design code for ageing](https://developers.weixin.qq.com/doc/service/en/guide/product/elderMedical/elderMedical-designstandards.html); [Frontiers 2022 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.931646/full); [Loughborough 2018](https://dspace.lboro.ac.uk/dspace-jspui/handle/2134/35436)) **[secondhand]**. Contrast 4.5:1 minimum, 7:1 preferred where feasible ([thisisglance](https://thisisglance.com/learning-centre/how-do-i-design-apps-for-elderly-people); [arXiv review of touchscreen guidelines for older adults](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.06317)).
- **Behaviour.** Consistency and standards first; error avoidance matters more than speed; guided, tightly directed flows; short walkthroughs over documentation; avoid pull-down menus and moving elements; tolerate punctuation variants; keep changes between versions small ([Ageing Lab UPM](https://ageinglab.ctb.upm.es/en/ux-and-usability-for-older-users-en); [Zurb summarising NN/g](https://radar.zurb.com/article/design-for-seniors-ship-a-better-product-for-everyone); [NN/g, Lexie Kane](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/author/lexie-kane/)).
- **Low literacy.** Text-only interfaces proved unusable for first-time low-literacy users; graphical cues, local language and shallow navigation work; 2025 e-government framework: simple navigation, mixed input/output modes, forgiving error recovery, cultural fit ([Medhi & Toyama, via research 09](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234829313_Designing_mobile_interfaces_for_novice_and_low-literacy_users); [Microsoft Research survey](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=167992); [CMS 2025 framework](https://openaccess.cms-conferences.org/publications/book/978-1-964867-75-5/article/978-1-964867-75-5_191)).

## 9. Vietnamese on screen

- Tone and vowel marks stack on one letter (ấ, ở, ữ); many Latin fonts cover simple accents but not these combinations; a lost mark changes meaning; fixed-height containers and tight line-height clip or collide marks; normalise strings to NFC and set `lang="vi"`; check that any font subset includes the Latin Extended Additional block ([Thy Hà interview on diacritics](https://typeelectives.substack.com/p/type-design-and-diacritics-a-conversation); [1StopAsia](https://www.1stopasia.com/blog/vietnamese-writing-systems-and-their-role-in-successful-localization-strategies/); [MotaWord](https://intel.motaword.com/languages/vietnamese-website-localization); [vietnamesetypography.com](https://vietnamesetypography.com/type-recommendations/)). No expansion table includes Vietnamese; measure with real strings; the risk is vertical, so give buttons line-height 1.3 or more and never set Vietnamese in all-caps (research 06 §6). Our bundled Noto Sans covers Vietnamese; the audit found uppercase eyebrow labels ("TUẦN NÀY ĐẾN GIỜ") and an 11 px tab label that wraps ("Hồ sơ kiểm tra lao động").
- Pseudo-localisation test strings should use real tone-marked letters ([TestParty i18n](https://testparty.ai/blog/accessibility-localization-internationalization-strategy)).

## 11. Onboarding and empty states

- Get people to the one action tied to retention first; treat a blank screen as onboarding: say what the area is for, show what it will look like, and offer the first action plus an import or sample path; checklists of three to seven tasks, easy wins first, with a progress indicator and a way to dismiss; two-thirds of a 15-company sample lead with a checklist; skip tours on phones ([Arcade onboarding playbook](https://www.arcade.software/post/saas-onboarding-complete-playbook); [Candu self-service onboarding](https://www.candu.ai/blog/the-complete-guide-to-self-service-saas-onboarding)) **[vendor sources]**.

## 13. Design rules for the revamp (cited by the plan as R1-R30)

| # | Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Every tappable thing looks tappable: filled or outlined buttons, underlined links, raised tiles; no ghost buttons for primary actions | §1 flat UI study |
| R2 | Every icon has a visible text label in the active language; no emoji glyphs | §1 icon usability |
| R3 | One filled primary button per screen, in the bottom third on phones, sticky when the page exceeds 1.5 screens | §3 |
| R4 | Touch targets 48×48 px minimum, 56 px for primaries, 64-72 px on the kiosk, 8 px gaps | §5 |
| R5 | Body text 16 px minimum; inputs 16 px or larger; kiosk names 28 px or larger; no text under 14 px except print | §3 iOS zoom, §8 |
| R6 | Colour never carries meaning alone: status = colour + icon + word; "owed" = red + alert icon + label | §2 WCAG 1.4.1 |
| R7 | Text contrast 4.5:1, borders and icons 3:1; hint text is text | §2 |
| R8 | Red is reserved for "owed by law" and the confirm step of destructive actions; clock-out, void, unpair, delete links are neutral or outlined until confirmed | §2 red money |
| R9 | Semantic colour tokens (ok, warn, owed, danger, info) defined once in CSS; components never use raw hues | §2 tokens |
| R10 | Light theme; optional dim-after-hours on the kiosk only | §2 dark mode |
| R11 | Feedback within 100 ms of a tap (pressed state), a pending state after 300 ms that stays until the response, nothing that flashes | §1 response times |
| R12 | Reversible actions act immediately and offer Undo for 8-10 s; only approve, mark paid, reopen, delete and unpair use a confirm step, and it is an inline two-press button with the amount, not a modal | §1 undo |
| R13 | Toasts confirm, never report errors; errors are inline next to the field or as a banner | §1 toasts |
| R14 | Bottom tab bar with four labelled tabs on phones (Home, Today, Pay, More); a labelled sidebar at 1024 px and up; no hamburger as the only navigation | §4 |
| R15 | Rare functions (settings, logins, audit export, language) live one level down under More | §4 progressive disclosure |
| R16 | Copy conventions people already know: Square's tile grid and tip presets, Homebase's PIN flow, iOS passcode dots, the OS share sheet | §4 Jakob, §10 |
| R17 | Numbers right-aligned with tabular figures; the lead number upper-left with its verdict; three to seven numbers per view | §6, §3 dashboards |
| R18 | Wide tables become cards on phones; the full table keeps a sticky header and a pinned first column on tablets and desktops | §6 |
| R19 | Forms are single column with labels above; placeholders are never labels; validate when a field is complete, next to the field, in words plus an icon | §7 |
| R20 | Two to five options are chips, segmented controls or radio cards, never a dropdown | §7 |
| R21 | Money fields: text input, `inputmode="decimal"`, visible `$`, cents stored; the tablet ticket builder uses an on-screen keypad | §7 money |
| R22 | Re-use what the person already entered: sticky technician, last service, last tip, last payment method, last pay method per technician | §4 WCAG 3.3.7 |
| R23 | Kiosk: idle reset 30 s with a countdown ring, one next step per screen, phone-layout PIN pad, shake and clear on a wrong PIN, no OS keyboard anywhere | §5 |
| R24 | Kiosk tiles fill the screen, carry an initials avatar, the name, a status colour, a status icon and the time since; the prompt is bilingual | §5, §10 avatars |
| R25 | The kiosk ships with a lockdown guide (Guided Access, screen pinning) and a fullscreen manifest for `/kiosk` | §5 lockdown |
| R26 | Vietnamese: line-height 1.3+ in buttons, no all-caps, NFC strings, `lang` attribute, layouts tested at 390 px with real Vietnamese labels | §9 |
| R27 | Skeletons only to hold layout on the pay week; spinners inside buttons for actions; optimistic append for tickets only | §1 skeletons, optimistic |
| R28 | Every empty state names the screen's job, shows the first action, and offers import or demo data; first run shows a four-step checklist | §11 |
| R29 | Statements read like the tech's own tally: tickets by day, tips on their own lines, plain-language arithmetic, no variable names | §10 Vietnamese apps, research 06 rule 4 |
| R30 | Keep changes between releases small and consistent; the owner's muscle memory is a feature | §8 older adults |
